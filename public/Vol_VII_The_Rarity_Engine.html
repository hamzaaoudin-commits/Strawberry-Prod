Scene 01 · Diagnostic
The Abundance Trap
002
Strawberry Prod.
Scene 01
The abundance trap.
Producing more no longer raises revenue. It lowers value.
"AI gave every operator infinite production capacity. Every founder can publish ten articles a week. Every studio can deliver double the work. Every product team can ship faster than ever.

And yet — prices are collapsing. Margins are evaporating. The market is paying less, not more, for the things we produce faster."
The Truth
Volume no longer
signals quality.
It signals desperation.
— ABUNDANCE IS THE NEW DISCOUNT.
Three Symptoms of a Brand Trapped in Abundance
01
Your offer is always available.
No waitlist. No closed window. No season.
Perpetually open offers signal that the supply is unlimited and the demand is uncertain. Buyers wait for discounts that always eventually come.
02
You publish constantly.
Daily. Multiple platforms. Every angle covered.
Constant publishing trains the market to value individual outputs at near-zero. The premium goes to the brand whose silence is interrupted, not the one that never stops.
03
Your delivery time is shrinking every quarter.
Because the market keeps demanding faster — and you keep agreeing.
Faster is not a brand. It is a race competitors with AI will inevitably win at lower margin. The brands that command premium are the ones that get slower deliberately, not faster.
"Abundance is now free.
Scarcity is the only thing left worth paying for."

Scene 03 · The Mechanics
Five Mechanics
004
Strawberry Prod.
Scene 03 — The Mechanics
The five mechanics of manufactured scarcity.
MECHANIC I · CAPACITY CAP
A fixed, published number of clients per year.
Not "limited slots" as a marketing line — an actual hard cap stated on the website. "We take twelve clients a year. Period." The cap is what makes the work scarce. The cap is what justifies the price.
MECHANIC II · CLOSED WINDOWS
Offers exist only during defined seasons.
Two intake windows per year. Three weeks each. After that, the door closes — visibly. Closed windows are broadcast, not hidden. The closure itself becomes content.
MECHANIC III · WAITLIST AUTHORITY
A public waitlist with visible length.
"Currently 47 founders on the waitlist for Q3." The visible number is the asset. It signals demand, social proof, and forces the next buyer to commit harder to skip the queue.
MECHANIC IV · WITHDRAWAL POWER
The willingness to remove the offer entirely.
Twice a year, publish that a service is being retired. Not paused. Retired. The removal is the marketing event. Buyers who waited learn the lesson, the market learns the rule: this brand does not always wait for you.
MECHANIC V · RELEASE RITUAL
Each new offer is a named event, not a launch.
Drop. Edition. Cohort. Cycle. The vocabulary is borrowed from luxury, not SaaS. Each release has a name, a number, a date, a closing date — and never reopens in identical form.
Scene 04 · Anatomy
The Release
005
Strawberry Prod.
Scene 04 — Anatomy
Anatomy of a scarce release.
Five elements. Each one borrowed from couture, not commerce.
R · 01
The Edition Number
"Edition 04. Twelve clients. Closes March 31." Numbers and dates do the heavy lifting. They convert availability into a clock — and clocks create urgency that prose cannot.
R · 02
The Cap
A clearly stated maximum — clients, units, weeks of work. Not "limited spaces." A specific integer. The number is the asset. The smaller and more believable, the higher the premium it sustains.
R · 03
The Ritual Date
Every edition opens and closes on a published date. The dates do not move. Ever. Moving the date once breaks the entire mechanic — and the market never quite believes you the next time.
R · 04
The Filter
An application or selection process — not a checkout button. The friction is the feature. The work of being chosen is what justifies the price and protects the standards.
R · 05
The Aftermath
When the window closes, publish the closing. "Edition 04 closed. Next opens September." Closure announcements perform better than launch announcements — because they teach the market the rule for next time.

Scene 06 · The Protocol
Rarity
007
Strawberry Prod.
Scene 06 — The Rarity Protocol
How to manufacture desire.
Restrict. Release. Reset. Three stages. One brand transformed from on-demand vendor into invitation-only institution.
01
Stage I · Restrict
Publish the cap — and never violate it.
Choose a number — clients, units, weeks of work — and state it publicly on the website, on every sales page, in every onboarding call. The cap is the constitution of rarity. Bending it once destroys the entire mechanic for the years that follow.
— Capacity audit & cap selection
— Public cap announcement
— Waitlist infrastructure
— Edition vocabulary lock
02
Stage II · Release
Treat every opening as a named event.
Edition number. Cohort name. Opening date. Closing date. Each release becomes a marketing event in itself, not a generic CTA. The pre-release window builds the demand; the closing creates the proof.
— Edition design & naming
— Pre-release content calendar
— Application & selection process
— Public closure announcement
03
Stage III · Reset
Withdraw, retire, and re-rare every twelve months.
Each year, one service is retired publicly. Each year, the cap may shrink. Each year, the brand's scarcity is renewed. The reset is what prevents rarity from drifting back into perceived abundance.
— Annual service retirement
— Cap review & reduction
— Waitlist communication ritual
— Price escalation cadence
